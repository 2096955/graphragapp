# Labelling rules

Every label in `app/testset.py` follows these rules. If you disagree with a label, change the
rule first, then every label it affects, so the set stays consistent.

Sections 6 and 7 (whole-group selection and preference fit) were added in version 1.1. Their
labels are written by hand like the rest, not generated by the pipeline's catalogue checks.

The catalogue holds 24 pollutants (CO2, CO2e 100-year, CO2e 20-year, CH4, N2O, PM2.5, PM10,
NOx, SO2, SOx, NH3, CO, NMVOCs, black carbon, organic carbon and nine heavy metals) with these
breakdowns: region, country or continent; month or year from 2015 to 2025; subsector or
macrosector. It holds nothing at city, street, station, day, hour or week level, no forecasts
and no other kinds of pollution.

## 1. Answer, clarify or reject (61 requests)

Apply in this order.

1. **Reject** if the request is off-topic; or asks only for pollutants the catalogue does not
   hold (ozone, SF6, NO2); or needs a level or period it does not hold (cities, days, 1850, 2040).
   A request that asks for some held and some unheld pollutants is not rejected: it is answered
   with the held ones, and the answer says which it left out (paper case 15 asks for NO2
   alongside CO2 and NOx).
2. **Clarify** if the request is in scope but names no pollutant and no general term, or gives
   no breakdown at all.
3. **Answer** otherwise.

What counts:

- "Pollution", "emissions", "air pollution", "data" and "everything" count as all pollutants.
  "Greenhouse gases", "particulate matter" and "heavy metals" count as those groups.
- A named place, period or sector counts as a breakdown at that member's level: "in Lombardy"
  is region, "from agriculture" is macrosector, "from road transport" is subsector.
- An explicitly named level beats a named member: "Asian countries" is country, not continent.
- A dimension named without a level takes the default: country, year, macrosector. "By
  geography and temporal dimensions" is country and year; "for sectors" and "by industry" are
  macrosector.

The 22 paper requests agree with the paper's outcomes except cases 18 and 20, which the paper
marks "Not sure" and these rules mark reject, because both need city-level data. The gates hold
31 answer, 16 clarify and 14 reject labels.

A version 1.1 patch relabelled case 15 as clarify so that the labels matched its rule-based
request parser. Version 1.2 restores the rule above: the paper answered case 15, and a label
should not be changed to agree with the code it is meant to test.

## 2. Pollutant relevance (132 pairs)

For each answerable request, up to three pollutants it asks for (yes) and up to three it does
not (no). Negatives are the likeliest confusions: the sibling pollutant (PM10 for PM2.5, SOx
for SO2, CO for CO2, N2O for NO2). Pairs whose answer is arguable are left out, for example
black carbon against a request for "particulate matter", or SO2 against "sulphur oxides".
Requests for all pollutants get positives only.

## 3. Breakdown level (93 questions)

For each answerable request and each of the three dimensions, the level from rule 1, or
"none" if the dimension is not mentioned.

## 4. Column mapping (80 columns)

Every column of the eight synthetic sources, labelled with the property it holds, or "none of
these" for identifiers, coordinates, populations and counts. Each question shows the right
answer, four likely confusions and "none of these", in a fixed shuffled order.

## 5. Entity match (82 pairs)

Two names of countries, regions, pollutants or sectors: "yes" if they name the same thing
under another name, spelling, language, abbreviation or code; "no" if they are different
things, including when one is part of the other (Lombardy and Milan) or a component of the
other (NO2 and NOx). Pairs where usage genuinely varies (Holland and the Netherlands, Great
Britain and the United Kingdom) are left out.

## 6. Whole-group selection (128 pairs)

Each of the 31 answerable requests gets four questions, one per group: greenhouse gases,
particulate matter, heavy metals and all pollutants. A named group is selected only when the
request names the group itself, not just some of its members. "All pollutants" is selected when
the request asks for pollution, emissions or data in general and names no pollutant or group.
`GROUP_GOLD` in `app/testset.py` lists the yes answers. A 32nd request, "Heavy metals by country
and year", adds the only heavy-metals yes. Most answers are no, so always saying no scores 93%.

## 7. Preference fit (8 decisions)

For "Official sources only", a single official source scores 3 (fully matches) and a single
research or commercial source scores 0 (does not match). Eight items can only catch gross
failures; they are not a preference benchmark.

## 8. Compliance filter (30 payloads)

Separate from the discovery test set above. The payloads and labels are in
`app/compliance_labels.py`; every name, address and number is invented, and e-mail addresses
use the reserved `.test` domain. The labels were written from this policy, not from the rules
backend, and the rules backend is scored against them like any other backend
(`python -m scripts.compliance_check`, or `POST /api/compliance/check`).

- **release** (9): nothing personal and nothing restricted. An organisation's role address
  (helpdesk@, info@) is not personal information. Asking how the filter works is not an attempt
  to get round it.
- **redact** (11): personal details that can be removed while the rest of the request still
  makes sense: a personal e-mail address, including one written out in words; a phone number;
  a government or health identifier (SSN, tax file number, Medicare number); a home address.
- **block** (10): an attempt to get round the filter, in any words ("ignore previous
  instructions", "skip the privacy check", "developer mode"); privileged or restricted material
  (attorney-client, internal only); personal data in bulk (a customer list, a staff directory).

When a payload fits two rules, block wins over redact, and redact over release.

Thirty payloads is a check, not a benchmark. It is there to show where exact rules stop working
(paraphrase, obfuscation, bulk data) and to keep "whether the filter was right" honest: payloads
outside this set have no label, and the eval view does not count them as right or wrong.

## 9. Claims graph (70 decisions and 10 retrieval questions)

Separate from the discovery test set. The corpus is `app/claims_corpus.py`: twelve publications
from three regulators, an industry body and a consumer group, all invented, with every name and
quote fictional. The labels are in `app/claims_labels.py`. Each claim has a topic and an aspect,
the point it addresses, and claims are compared only within one aspect.

- **Does the quote support the claim?** (22): yes when the quote says what the claim says, in
  these or other words; no when the claim overstates, weakens or changes it. X01 turns
  "we encourage" into "must", and X02 turns "must validate" into "may decide whether to
  validate". X03 quotes text that is not in the document, so the mechanical check rejects it and
  it is not labelled.
- **Same claim?** (39 pairs: every pair on the same aspect from different documents): yes only
  when both statements make the same claim with the same force. A statement that goes further,
  or less far, is a different claim: "should give customers a way to ask for a review" and "must
  review on request" are different claims. Four pairs are yes.
- **How did the position change?** (9 pairs: consecutive claims by the same person on the same
  aspect): same (the same position restated, including a proposal becoming final), stronger
  (further in the same direction: encouraged, then expected, then required), weaker (relaxed:
  should not, then may), opposite (reversed: opposes a requirement, then supports one).
- **Retrieval questions** (10): gold is the set of claims that answer the question, or, for the
  two questions no claim answers, a phrase from the passage that does. One question asks for a
  position as of a date; claims made after that date are not part of its answer.

Seventy decisions are a check, not a benchmark, and the corpus was written together with its
labels, so treat the numbers as a demonstration of the method rather than a measurement.
